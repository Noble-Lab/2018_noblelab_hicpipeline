Quality control statistics to look at for small genome Hi-C data

Last updated: 2016/04/07 by Kate

===

Interchromasomal contact probability (ICP) and percent of long-range contacts (PLRC)

- These are two statistics that can help convince you that your data is good
- note that the thresholds of 1kb and 20kb were chosen for yeast/plasmodium and may not be applicable to all genomes
- ICP: Kalhor et al 2011
- ICP = total number of interchromosomal contacts / total number of intrachromosomal contacts >1 kb
- Small ICP values indicate a better signal to noise ratio, since noise should be dominated by interchromosomal contacts
- PLRC = total number of interchromosomal contacts and intrachromosomal contacts >20 kb / number of raw reads
- larger PLRC values indicate more information about nonadjacent chromatin contacts
- See values for Plasmodium data in the Supplement of Ay 2013

Power Law fit

- e.g. Figure 1B of Ay 2013
- There should be an exponential relationship between how close two loci are in terms of genomic distance, and the probability of contacting
- This makes sure that the relationship between contact probability and genomic distance is what we expect
- fit-hi-c will generate these numbers
